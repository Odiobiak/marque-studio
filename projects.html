Editorial Portfolio

Quiet luxury for a data and AI engineer, with a live assistant tucked inside. Serif headlines, warm palette, and a terminal motif that nods to the craft.

This is the site that proved the idea to me. If a live AI concierge can hold its own here, it'll hold its own on yours.Odiche Obiakarije, Founder's note
Portfolio · AI Visit live ↗
The brief

An engineer with genuinely deep experience, and a CV that read like everyone else's. The work was strong. The first impression was not. It needed to say "this person builds serious things" before a single word was read.

What we did

We went editorial rather than technical: a serif headline, a warm restrained palette, and generous space. Then one small rebellion, a terminal motif blinking away in the hero, so the craft shows without shouting. The résumé became a story instead of a list.

The clever bit

We built a live AI assistant into the page, trained on his own work. Visitors can ask it anything about his experience and get a real answer, at any hour. It is the same technology we now offer as Marque Intelligence, and this was where we proved it.

Dual-Mode Personal Brand

One profile with two personalities. A single switch flips the whole site between a buttoned-up tech identity and a looser creative one.

I gave them a vague idea of what I wanted, and somehow they built exactly what I didn't know I needed.Freke, client
Personal brand Visit live ↗
The brief

Freke had a problem a lot of people quietly have. Two real careers, one name. The tech side needed credibility and structure. The creative side needed room to breathe. Picking one meant hiding half of who they were.

What we did

Rather than compromise into something beige, we built two sites in one. A single switch in the corner flips the entire identity: layout, palette, tone, and the order of the work. Same person, two honest first impressions.

The clever bit

The transition is deliberately smooth rather than a page reload, so the flip itself becomes the memorable moment. Send it to a recruiter or a creative director and they each land on the version that speaks to them.

03

Destination Wedding

A two-city celebration across Seville and Lagos, with our story, travel notes, RSVP, and registry. Shown here privately, out of respect for the couple.

Omg, when we received the preview we were amazed! All we did was give them an idea, and they absolutely delivered.Sope & Emmanuel, clients
Wedding · Events Link kept private
The brief

Two celebrations, two continents, one guest list trying to keep up. Seville for some, Lagos for others, many travelling to both. The free wedding-site templates could not handle it, and none of them looked like the couple.

What we did

We gave each city its own section with its own schedule, travel notes, and dress code, then tied it together with a custom monogram and the couple's own hashtag. Guests land once and immediately find their half of the story.

The clever bit

RSVP and registry live in the same flow, so nobody hunts through emails to reply or send a gift. The couple stopped fielding the same four questions over and over, which was the real point.

04

Festival Concept

A studio concept for a summer music weekender. Bold type, kinetic motion, and a lineup that reveals itself as you scroll. Made to show range.

Event · Concept In development
The brief we set ourselves

A three-day music weekender with no budget for a big agency and no patience for a generic ticket page. Could a festival site feel like the festival itself, loud, fast, and a little chaotic, without becoming unusable?

The approach

Oversized type that fills the screen, motion that reacts as you scroll, and a lineup that reveals itself stage by stage rather than dumping a wall of names. The schedule stays ruthlessly simple underneath the noise.

Why it is here

Most of our work is quiet and elegant. This one exists to show the other end of the range, that we can do bold and kinetic just as carefully when the occasion calls for it.

Ember & Rye A SUPPER CLUB · FIVE SEATS, ONE TABLE
05

Ember & Rye

A studio concept for an intimate supper club. Warm, low-lit typography and a booking flow built to feel like a reservation, not a form.

Restaurant · Concept In development
The brief we set ourselves

A supper club with five seats and one table. The website has an odd job here: it must feel exclusive enough to be worth chasing, while still making it genuinely easy to get a seat.

The approach

Low light, warm brass, and generous space, so the page feels like the room. Menus are written as short paragraphs rather than lists, because the appeal is the evening, not the ingredients.

The clever bit

The booking flow is designed to read like a reservation, not a form. Fewer fields, warmer wording, and a confirmation that sounds like a person rather than a receipt.

Amara Cole PORTRAIT & LIFESTYLE PHOTOGRAPHER
06

Amara Cole

A studio concept for a portrait photographer's personal brand. Soft, editorial, and built to let the work do the talking.

Personal brand · Concept In development
The brief we set ourselves

A portrait photographer whose work is the whole argument. Most photography sites bury the images under navigation, sliders, and text nobody reads. The challenge was to design something that mostly gets out of the way.

The approach

A soft blush palette, one quiet serif, and images given room to be large. Words are kept to a minimum and placed where they support the photographs rather than compete with them.

Why it is here

It is the restraint piece. Proof that knowing what to leave out is as much of the job as knowing what to add, especially for anyone whose portfolio is visual.
